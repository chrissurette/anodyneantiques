Estate services · Cape Coral, Florida

Estate services in Cape Coral.

Help settling a Cape Coral home — a canal house in the older southeast Cape, a newer build out toward Burnt Store Road, or a condo at the Yacht Club — with a free walk-through, a written evaluation, and honest options for everything in it.

Across the river, on the same run as Fort Myers

Cape Coral is the far end of our territory — about an hour from Shirley Street by I-75 and one of the bridges over the Caloosahatchee — and we go often enough that a walk-through can usually be fitted into the next trip north, or made specially when a closing demands it. We cover the whole Cape, from the Yacht Club and the older neighborhoods off Cape Coral Parkway to Burnt Store Road, plus Matlacha and Pine Island beyond.

A different kind of house

The Cape was platted in the late 1950s as one enormous subdivision of canal lots, and it still is: hundreds of miles of canals, mostly single-family homes, very few gates, and a lot of driveways a truck can actually reach. That makes the logistics simpler than Naples or Marco — no elevator reservations, no vendor registration — and it makes a traditional estate sale possible in many neighborhoods where it isn't elsewhere. When a Cape house is a genuine fit for one, we'll say so. When it isn't, our private route — walk-through, written evaluation, buyout, consignment or auction — is faster and keeps the public out of the home. The routes compared.

Who built these houses, and what they left

The Cape's first residents came in the 1960s and '70s — Midwestern retirees, working families, boaters, and a remarkable number of Germans, who made the city one of the largest German-speaking communities in Florida. Their estates are unlike Naples estates. We see Continental silver marked 800 and 835, Meissen, Rosenthal and Hutschenreuther porcelain, Black Forest carvings and clocks, beer steins and Hummels by the shelf, Bavarian furniture, and European paintings brought over decades ago. We also see the honest mid-century American furniture of the early Cape, marine and fishing gear worth more than the family expects, tools, and collections built over a long retirement. Some of it is modest; some of it is exactly the kind of thing collectors in Munich or Milwaukee are looking for, and we know the difference.

The newer estates — homes built in the last two decades along the western canals and out toward the Burnt Store corridor — follow the familiar pattern: the good pieces are what came from the northern house.

Working around the Cape's realities

Tolls and bridges, long drives across a very large city, homes that have been through storms, and — commonly — a house that sat empty for a while before the family could get to it. We'll tell you honestly what humidity and time have done to a piece, and what's still worth selling. Out-of-state heirs are the norm here; we coordinate with a neighbor, realtor or attorney and handle the rest by email and phone.

Firearms turn up in Cape estates more than most; we don't buy them and will point you to a licensed dealer. Coins, gold, jewelry and watches go to our sister shop, Naples Estate Jewelry, in the same trip.

Cape Coral questions

Is Cape Coral really in your area?

Yes. It's the far end of it, so we group Cape visits with Fort Myers trips — tell us your dates and we'll fit the house into the next one, or make a special trip if there's a closing. The walk-through is free either way.

My parents brought a house full of German porcelain and silver over. Is it worth anything?

Often, yes. Continental silver marked 800 or 835, Meissen and other good porcelain, Black Forest clocks and carvings all have real markets — some local, some in Europe and the Midwest, which is where auction placement comes in. Photograph the marks on the underside and send them to us. A guide to European silver marks.

Can we hold an estate sale in the Cape?

In many Cape neighborhoods, yes — there's no HOA to forbid it. We don't run them, but if the house is a genuine fit we'll say so and refer a reputable company. For the good pieces, we'd still recommend evaluating first so they don't go cheap on a Saturday morning.

The house has been empty since the storm. Is anything salvageable?

Frequently more than you'd think — silver, porcelain, glass, clocks and metalwork survive what fabric and veneer don't. We'll tell you honestly, piece by piece, during the walk-through.

Do you buy guns, coins or boats?

Not firearms or boats — we'll refer you. Coins and gold go to our sister shop, Naples Estate Jewelry. Marine antiques, instruments, charts and good tackle we'll evaluate and buy.
