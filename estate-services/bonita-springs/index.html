Twenty minutes up the Tamiami Trail

Bonita Springs is our nearest neighbor to the north — twenty to twenty-five minutes from Shirley Street by US-41 or I-75 — and we're there most weeks. Close enough that a walk-through can usually happen within a couple of days, and that a piece can come back to the shop the same afternoon.

Gated communities and the front gate

A large share of Bonita's homes sit behind a gate: Bonita Bay, Pelican Landing, Spanish Wells, Palmira, Hunters Ridge, Worthington, Highland Woods, Bonita National and the rest. Each has its own routine — a name on the visitor list, vendor registration, rules about which days a truck may come through and where it may park, and an HOA with opinions about anything that looks like a sale. We handle the registration, ask you for the gate or HOA contact on the first call, and schedule pickups inside the community's rules. If you've inherited a home in a 55+ community that forbids estate sales outright, our way of working — nothing public in the house — is usually the only one the rules allow. How the routes compare.

Bonita's long-time residents

Many Bonita Springs homes were bought in the 1980s and '90s by people retiring from Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland, Boston and Pittsburgh — and a good number of them brought the whole northern house with them. That means the dining room is mahogany, there is a sterling flatware service in the sideboard, oriental rugs are under the furniture, and the oil paintings on the walls came from a grandparent. Old Bonita — the streets around the Imperial River and the downtown — has older Florida houses with older Florida things. Barefoot Beach and the Bonita Beach condos have the same elevator and move-window rules as Marco Island's towers, and we plan for them the same way.

What we find in Bonita Springs estates

Dining suites and secretaries, complete sterling sets in patterns by Gorham, Towle, Wallace and Reed & Barton, tea services, Lenox and Royal Doulton, mantel clocks, oil paintings and good prints, Chinese export porcelain, rugs — and, reliably, the best pieces tucked in the guest-room closet where nobody has looked in a decade. Jewelry, gold, watches and coins go to our sister shop, Naples Estate Jewelry, in the same visit.

Splitting things among the family first

A common Bonita request: three siblings in three states, each wanting certain pieces, and nobody sure what anything is worth. We'll evaluate the whole house before anything is divided, so the split can be fair by value and not just by sentiment — and then handle whatever is left with a single plan.

Bonita Springs questions

Can you register at the gate yourselves?

Yes. Give us the community's gate or management contact and we'll handle vendor registration, the visitor list and any insurance paperwork before the visit.

Our parents' community doesn't allow estate sales. What can we do?

Exactly what we do everywhere: a private walk-through, a written evaluation, then buyout, consignment or auction placement — with no public sale in the house. HOA rules against tag sales don't stop any of it.

We need the house empty before a closing in three weeks. Realistic?

Yes, for Bonita Springs. We'd buy the general contents outright, place only the few pieces that truly justify a longer route, and coordinate donation and clean-out for the rest. Tell us the date on the first call.

Can you help us divide things fairly among siblings first?

Gladly. We'll evaluate the contents before anything is divided so each person knows what the pieces are worth, then handle the remainder with one plan.

Do you also cover Estero?

Yes — Estero, San Carlos Park and the Coconut Point area are on the same run. Call and tell us where the house is.
