When auction beats a sale

Most things sell best locally and quickly. But every so often an estate turns up something with a signature, a maker's mark, a provenance or a rarity that puts it in a different category — the sort of piece that two determined collectors in London and Hong Kong might bid against each other for. That piece should not be sold across a counter in Naples. It should be placed in the right sale, at the right house, with the right estimate, and given the room it deserves.

Knowing which is which is most of the skill. We'll tell you straight when something is a local sale, and when it isn't.

What we do for you

  • Identify the candidates. During an estate walk-through or from your photographs, we flag the pieces with genuine auction potential.
  • Research and document. Marks, signatures, provenance, comparable results. We photograph properly and write it up.
  • Choose the right sale. Regional house or international; general sale or a specialist department for silver, furniture, paintings, Asian art or design. The right room matters more than the biggest name.
  • Handle the submission. Condition reports, specialist correspondence, estimates and reserves — negotiated on your behalf, explained in plain English.
  • Manage the logistics. Packing, insured shipping, delivery deadlines and catalogue proofs.
  • See it through. We track the sale, reconcile the settlement, and follow up on anything unsold.

What it costs

Auction houses charge the seller a commission and, sometimes, fees for photography, insurance and shipping. Our role and our fee are set out in writing before anything is consigned, alongside the house's terms, so you can see the whole picture on one page. No surprises at settlement.

Because we negotiate as a repeat consignor, the terms we obtain are frequently better than an individual would be offered walking in cold — and we'll show you the arithmetic.

How long it takes

Major sales run on a calendar: consignment deadlines, cataloguing, previews, the sale itself, and settlement some weeks after. Three to six months from consignment to payment is typical; specialist sales can be longer. If an estate can't wait that long, we'll say so and suggest the faster route instead.

A word on discretion

We don't discuss whose estate a piece came from, and we don't advertise our auction relationships. If you'd prefer that a piece be catalogued as "property of a Naples collector," that's easily arranged.

Questions people ask

How do I know if something is "auction-worthy"?

Send us photographs — front, back, any marks or signatures — or bring it in. We'll tell you, free of charge, whether it belongs at auction, on our floor, or with a buyer today. Most things are the latter two; the exceptions are worth finding.

Which auction houses do you work with?

We work with a range of regional and international houses and choose by the piece, not by the name. We don't publish those relationships, but we'll explain exactly why we're recommending a particular sale for your object, and you'll see the house's terms before you sign anything.

Can't I just contact an auction house myself?

You can. What we add is judgement about which house and which sale, negotiated terms, properly documented submissions, handled logistics, and a single person accountable to you from consignment to settlement. For an estate with several candidates, or an heir who lives out of state, that tends to be worth a great deal.

What if the piece doesn't sell?

It happens. We'll have discussed a sensible reserve beforehand, and if a piece is bought in we'll advise on the next step — a second sale, a different house, consignment through the shop, or a direct offer from us.

Do you handle the shipping and insurance?

Yes. We arrange packing, insured transport and delivery to the house's deadline, and we keep the paperwork for your file.
