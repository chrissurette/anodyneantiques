Paintings, prints, and the frames around them.

Oil paintings, portraits, watercolors, drawings and prints — signed or not — along with period gilt frames and mirrors. Send us the front, the signature and the back, and we'll tell you what you have and what to do with it.

What we buy

  • Oil paintings — portraits, landscapes, marine and sporting pictures, still lifes, interiors; European and American, 18th to mid-20th century. Signed or anonymous: we buy the picture, not the name.
  • Works on paper — watercolors, drawings, engravings, etchings, hand-colored botanical and natural-history plates, early maps.
  • Florida art — landscape paintings by the Highwaymen and other Florida painters, early views of Naples and the Gulf coast, and prints and maps of Florida. These turn up in local estates more than anywhere and are collected seriously.
  • Period frames — gilt, carved, gesso, empty or not. Designers buy them faster than the pictures.
  • Mirrors — gilt, painted, Venetian, trumeau, and plain looking glasses with age.
  • Sculpture and bronzes, when the quality is there.

Signed, unsigned, and the question of who

A signature is the beginning of the research, not the end of it. We look at the picture first — quality, subject, period, condition, frame — and then at the name, the labels and stamps on the back, and what comparable works have actually sold for. When a painting turns out to be by a listed artist with an auction record, it usually belongs at a specialist sale rather than across our counter, and we'll say so and place it for you. When it's a good anonymous portrait with presence, it belongs on our wall, and we'll make you a fair offer for it.

Photograph the back

Half the story of a painting is on its reverse: gallery labels, exhibition stickers, old auction lot tags, inscriptions, canvas stamps, the stretcher construction. Photograph the front in even light without flash, the signature close up, the back in full, and any labels close enough to read. That's usually enough for a straight opinion by email.

Condition, honestly

Florida is hard on pictures: humidity, sun and salt air cause flaking, foxing on paper, warped panels and bloomed varnish. Old relining and restoration are normal and not necessarily a problem; recent amateur cleaning is. Tell us what you know, and don't attempt to clean or "touch up" anything before we see it — the same rule as silver and furniture.

Whole collections

For a house of pictures — a lifetime's collecting, or a wall of family portraits — we come to you, photograph and evaluate every piece, and give you a written plan: what we'd buy, what belongs on consignment, what belongs at auction, and what should stay in the family. That's part of our estate service, and it's free.

Questions people ask

Do you buy unsigned paintings?

Yes. Quality, subject, period and condition matter more to us than a name. A fine anonymous portrait in a good frame is exactly what our decorator clients want.

How do I know if my painting is valuable?

Send photographs of the front, the signature and the back. We'll research it and tell you honestly — and if it merits a specialist auction, we'll say so rather than buy it cheaply.

We think it's a Highwaymen painting. Can you tell?

Usually, from the style, the materials and the back of the board. Photograph everything and send it over; if it's the real thing, it's a piece we'd place at auction. More on Florida art in Fort Myers estates.

Do you buy prints and reproductions?

Original prints — engravings, etchings, lithographs, hand-colored plates — yes. Modern decorative reproductions, giclées and posters, no, and we'll tell you the difference.

Do you buy empty frames?

Gladly. Period gilt frames are among the first things to leave the shop.

Always acquiring

Have something with a past?

We buy single pieces and entire estates — antiques, silver, art, and furniture. Honest evaluations, free of charge and free of pressure.
